# Expected Answer — Case 001

## Executive conclusion

July revenue is **₹653,600**. Of this amount, **₹623,200** is an unconditional receivable for 82 approved days and **₹30,400** is a contract asset for four delivered days whose billing right remains conditional on administrative timesheet approval.

The answer depends on the stated fact that customer approval is administrative rather than substantive acceptance and that reliable evidence supports delivery before 31 July.

## 1. Performance obligation

The daily data-engineering services are distinct, substantially the same and transferred using the same pattern. They are therefore accounted for as a **series of distinct services forming one performance obligation**, consistent with the series guidance in Ind AS 115.

This conclusion should be reconsidered if the contract includes separately identifiable deliverables, implementation milestones, licences, options or other promises not included in the case facts.

## 2. Timing of revenue recognition

Revenue is recognised **over time** because Orion receives and consumes the benefits as TechAxis performs each day of service. The relevant over-time basis is the customer-receives-and-consumes criterion.

Invoice timing does not determine revenue timing. The analysis follows transfer of service and enforceable entitlement.

## 3. Measure of progress

An **output method based on eligible consultant-days delivered** faithfully depicts performance because:

- the consideration is stated per consultant-day;
- each day is substantially the same service;
- delivered days can be evidenced; and
- the measure excludes work not performed.

A time-elapsed method would be weaker here because staffing days, rather than the passage of calendar time, drive value and consideration.

## 4. Revenue calculation

| Component | Calculation | Amount |
|---|---:|---:|
| Approved days — gross | 82 × ₹8,000 | ₹656,000 |
| Pending-approval days — gross | 4 × ₹8,000 | ₹32,000 |
| **Gross consideration** | 86 × ₹8,000 | **₹688,000** |
| Service credit | ₹688,000 × 5% | **(₹34,400)** |
| **July revenue** | ₹688,000 − ₹34,400 | **₹653,600** |

The net eligible rate is ₹8,000 × 95% = **₹7,600 per delivered day**.

The 5% service credit is variable consideration that reduces the transaction price. Because July uptime is known at the reporting date and the credit is enforceable, no unresolved estimation remains for this condition.

## 5. Receivable and contract asset

| Classification | Calculation | Amount |
|---|---:|---:|
| Receivable — 82 approved days | 82 × ₹7,600 | **₹623,200** |
| Contract asset — 4 pending days | 4 × ₹7,600 | **₹30,400** |
| **Total recognised** | 86 × ₹7,600 | **₹653,600** |

The approved amount is a receivable because the right to consideration is unconditional other than passage of time. The pending amount is a contract asset because the billing right remains conditional on obtaining administrative approval, even though the related service has already transferred.

## 6. Journal entry at 31 July 2026

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Trade receivable | ₹623,200 | — |
| Contract asset — unbilled services | ₹30,400 | — |
| Revenue from services | — | ₹653,600 |
| **Total** | **₹653,600** | **₹653,600** |

No separate expense or provision is recorded for the service credit because revenue has already been measured net of the enforceable credit.

## 7. Entry when the four days are approved

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Trade receivable | ₹30,400 | — |
| Contract asset — unbilled services | — | ₹30,400 |

This is a balance-sheet reclassification. No additional revenue is recognised because revenue was recorded when the service transferred.

## 8. Evidence to retain

- Executed contract and rate card
- Clauses governing timesheet approval and customer acceptance
- Consultant timesheets and daily work records
- System-access or activity logs
- Deliverable or ticket evidence
- Project-manager certification
- Evidence that the customer approver was unavailable
- Subsequent customer approval
- Uptime report and service-credit calculation
- Reconciliation from delivered days to revenue, contract asset and invoice

Subsequent approval is corroborative evidence; it should not replace evidence that the services were delivered before the reporting date.

## 9. Review triggers

Escalate or change the accounting if:

- approval is a substantive acceptance condition;
- evidence for the four days is incomplete or contradictory;
- the customer disputes the work;
- the service-credit clause has caps, exclusions or a different allocation basis;
- the contract contains additional promises or material rights;
- termination or enforceability clauses affect the right to payment;
- collectability is no longer probable;
- the approved amount is not yet an unconditional right; or
- tax, foreign-exchange or financing terms omitted from the case become relevant.

## 10. Facts, assumptions and judgement

**Facts:** 86 days were delivered; 82 were approved; four awaited administrative approval; July uptime was 99.2%; a 5% credit applies below 99.5%.

**Assumptions supplied by the case:** the contract is enforceable, collection is probable, no financing component exists and taxes are excluded.

**Professional judgement:** the series conclusion, over-time criterion, output method and contract-asset classification depend on the contract language and evidence supporting the administrative nature of approval.
