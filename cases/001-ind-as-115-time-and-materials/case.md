# Case 001 — Ind AS 115 Time-and-Materials Services

## Case purpose

This synthetic case evaluates whether an AI system or finance professional can distinguish revenue recognition from billing, analyse a series of daily services, account for variable consideration, identify a contract asset and produce reconciling journal entries.

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**Reporting framework:** Ind AS 115  
**Reporting date:** 31 July 2026  
**Currency:** Indian rupees  
**All entities and facts are fictional.**

## Scenario

TechAxis Solutions Private Limited provides data-engineering support to Orion Retail Limited under a time-and-materials contract running from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027.

The finance team is closing July 2026 and must determine revenue, receivable and contract-asset balances.

## Contract facts

1. TechAxis charges ₹8,000 for each consultant-day of service.
2. The customer receives and consumes the benefit as each day of service is delivered.
3. Daily services are substantially the same and follow the same transfer pattern.
4. Customer-approved timesheets are invoiceable immediately. Invoices are issued monthly.
5. Four additional days were delivered before 31 July. Customer approval was pending only because the authorised approver was on leave.
6. Approval is an administrative verification, not a substantive customer-acceptance condition.
7. Internal access logs, daily work records and the project manager's certification support delivery of the four pending days.
8. A service credit of 5% applies to July eligible fees if monthly system uptime is below 99.5%.
9. July uptime was 99.2%. The 5% credit is therefore known and enforceable at the reporting date.
10. The credit applies proportionately to every eligible consultant-day.
11. Amounts exclude GST and income taxes.
12. Collection is considered probable and there is no financing component.

## Required analysis

Prepare a concise accounting memo that:

1. Identifies the performance obligation or obligations.
2. Determines whether revenue is recognised over time or at a point in time and explains why.
3. Selects an appropriate method for measuring progress.
4. Calculates gross consideration, the service credit and July revenue.
5. Separates the amount that is an unconditional receivable from the contract asset.
6. Provides the 31 July journal entry.
7. Provides the reclassification entry when the four pending days are approved.
8. Lists the evidence that should be retained for the pending days.
9. Identifies conditions that would change the conclusion or require senior review.
10. Clearly separates stated facts, assumptions and professional judgement.

## Evaluation constraints

- Do not equate invoicing with revenue recognition.
- Do not recognise more than the amount supported by delivered services.
- Do not ignore the enforceable service credit.
- Do not treat administrative approval as substantive acceptance without evidence.
- Show every calculation and ensure the entries reconcile.
