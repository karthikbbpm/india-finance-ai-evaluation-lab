# Evaluation Rubric — Case 001

## Scoring overview

| Dimension | Maximum points |
|---|---:|
| Performance-obligation analysis | 15 |
| Timing of revenue recognition | 15 |
| Measure of progress | 10 |
| Revenue and service-credit calculation | 25 |
| Receivable and contract-asset classification | 15 |
| Journal entries and reconciliation | 10 |
| Evidence, assumptions and review triggers | 10 |
| **Total** | **100** |

## 1. Performance-obligation analysis — 15 points

- 10 points: Identifies the daily services as a series of distinct services forming one performance obligation.
- 3 points: Explains that services are substantially the same.
- 2 points: Explains that the transfer pattern is the same.

**Zero for this section** if the answer creates 86 unrelated performance obligations without analysing the series guidance.

## 2. Timing of revenue recognition — 15 points

- 10 points: Concludes that revenue is recognised over time because the customer simultaneously receives and consumes the benefits.
- 3 points: Separates revenue recognition from invoicing.
- 2 points: Recognises that administrative approval does not automatically defer revenue.

**Major error:** Point-in-time recognition solely because invoices are monthly.

## 3. Measure of progress — 10 points

- 6 points: Selects delivered consultant-days as an output measure.
- 2 points: Explains why the method depicts performance.
- 2 points: Excludes undelivered work and avoids a calendar-time shortcut.

## 4. Revenue and service-credit calculation — 25 points

- 5 points: Approved gross amount = ₹656,000.
- 3 points: Pending gross amount = ₹32,000.
- 4 points: Gross consideration = ₹688,000.
- 5 points: Service credit = ₹34,400.
- 5 points: Net July revenue = ₹653,600.
- 3 points: Net eligible day rate = ₹7,600.

Award partial credit only where the workings are visible and the error can be isolated.

## 5. Classification — 15 points

- 7 points: Receivable = ₹623,200 for approved days.
- 6 points: Contract asset = ₹30,400 for delivered days awaiting administrative approval.
- 2 points: Explains the conditional-versus-unconditional distinction.

**Major error:** Recording the pending amount as receivable without analysing the billing condition, or omitting it from revenue solely because it is unbilled.

## 6. Journal entries and reconciliation — 10 points

- 5 points: Correct 31 July entry.
- 3 points: Correct subsequent reclassification from contract asset to receivable.
- 2 points: Entries balance and reconcile to ₹653,600.

## 7. Evidence, assumptions and review triggers — 10 points

- 4 points: Lists appropriate delivery and approval evidence.
- 2 points: Identifies the uptime report and service-credit evidence.
- 2 points: Distinguishes facts, assumptions and judgement.
- 2 points: Flags substantive acceptance, disputes, weak evidence, clause differences or enforceability concerns for review.

## Overall performance bands

| Score | Assessment |
|---|---|
| 90–100 | Expert-quality: technically sound, fully reconciled and review-ready |
| 75–89 | Strong: correct conclusion with limited omissions |
| 60–74 | Usable with review: core conclusion mostly correct but important gaps remain |
| 40–59 | Weak: partial understanding with material errors or missing controls |
| Below 40 | Unreliable: conclusion should not be used |

## Score caps for critical failures

Apply the lowest relevant cap:

- **Maximum 65:** Correct revenue number reached without explaining the recognition basis.
- **Maximum 55:** Ignores the service credit or recognises only invoiced revenue.
- **Maximum 45:** Uses point-in-time recognition without addressing the over-time criterion.
- **Maximum 40:** Journal entries do not balance or materially exceed delivered consideration.
- **Maximum 30:** Invents facts, hides assumptions, or claims certainty despite contradictory evidence.

## Common failure modes

1. Treating customer approval as automatically substantive.
2. Recognising only 82 approved days and omitting supported delivered services.
3. Recognising all gross fees without the service credit.
4. Recording all 86 days as an unconditional receivable.
5. Recording the service credit as an operating expense instead of reducing revenue.
6. Re-recognising revenue when the pending days are later approved.
7. Providing paragraph references without applying them to the facts.
8. Producing a correct narrative with calculations that do not reconcile.
